01 UAV & Computer Vision

TEKNOFEST Fighter UAV

Anafarta UAV Team · May 2025 – Aug 2026. I owned the aircraft-detection workflow end to end: dataset preparation, annotation, YOLO training and evaluation, then real-time deployment on the Jetson that flies inside the aircraft.

Dynamic ROI: keeping a distant target

At a few hundred metres an aircraft is only a few pixels wide, so a detector that sees the whole frame often finds it once and loses it on the next frame. Left: full-frame inference. Right: after the first detection, a padded 16:9 region (blue) around the last known position is cropped and run through the model. The target keeps enough pixels to be detected frame after frame.

  • Full-frame scan until the first hit
  • ROI recentred on every detection, forced to 16:9
  • Falls back to full frame after a short timeout
  • Optional S + N ensemble rejects single-model false positives

02 Edge AI System

How the aircraft sees, decides and reports

The whole onboard stack around the detector: camera to TensorRT on a Jetson Orin NX, lock-on and guidance over MAVLink to the flight controller, live video and a two-way data link to the ground station, and the competition server behind it. Pick a flow, or click any block.

The onboard loop

One CSI camera feeds a single GStreamer graph. The same frames are used for detection, for the overlay the pilots watch, and for the recording. The detector's output closes the loop through guidance commands to the autopilot.

  1. DetectYOLO11 on TensorRT, 720p inference branch
  2. ZoomDynamic ROI crop around the last hit
  3. TrackIoU gate, 0.8 s coasting, re-acquire after 3 frames
  4. Lock4.0 s continuous in the zone, ≥5% of frame, 0.2 s tolerance
  5. GuidePixel → line of sight → GPS waypoint, 10 Hz; speed from box size
  6. ReportLock-on packet to the competition server
